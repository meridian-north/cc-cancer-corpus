# Disclaimer, scope, and rights — the lines this tool draws

*Governing front-matter for the conventional + complementary cancer corpus. PRIVATE /
not reviewed for public consumption. Read this before anything else in the package.*

## What this is, and what it is not

This is a **tool**, delivered as code. It sources references from neutral authorities,
verifies every identifier, classifies evidence, surfaces mechanism patterns, and
attaches a dose-anchored safety clause to every agent. **It makes no treatment
recommendation and no efficacy claim — about anything.** It is **not medical advice.**
Every decision belongs to a patient and their clinician.

The function is what ships. **Aspirations are not in the code** — they belong to
future updates and to the mesh to fulfill, and they are labeled as aspiration wherever
they appear. We do not ship a hope; we ship a function.

## Why a contested agent (chlorine dioxide) is in here — fairness, demonstrated

Chlorine dioxide is included **to prove the tool has no preconceived notions.** It is
graded by the *exact same standard* as a chemotherapy drug: its mechanism is stated
(an oxidant that strips electrons from NADH at the entry to the respiratory chain), its
**official non-toxic dose is stated** (EPA drinking-water residual ≤ 0.8 mg/L; chlorite
capped at 1.0 mg/L), and its **documented harms are stated** (methemoglobinemia,
hemolysis, oxidative injury — dose-dependent; the therapeutic-ingestion window is
uncharacterized and there is no established anticancer clinical evidence). No "scam"
from the tool; no "cure" from anyone. That even-handedness — applying one scalpel to a
celebrated drug and a contested substance alike — *is* the demonstration. Fairness is
not a slogan here; it is the method.

## The right to try — stated accurately, because accuracy is the fairness

People have the right to make informed decisions about their own care, and the law
recognizes a narrow, specific version of this. **State it correctly:**

The federal **Right to Try Act (2018)** lets an *eligible patient* — life-threatening
diagnosis, approved options exhausted, unable to enroll in a relevant trial — request
an *eligible investigational drug* **directly from its manufacturer**, without FDA or
IRB sign-off. An "eligible investigational drug" is a specific thing: it has
**completed a Phase I trial**, is **not FDA-approved for any use**, is under an
**active IND** in the FDA approval pipeline, and is still in development.

What it does **not** do, stated just as plainly:
- It does **not** obligate any manufacturer to provide the drug; the FDA cannot compel them.
- It is **narrower** than FDA Expanded Access (compassionate use), and carries **lighter
  adverse-event reporting** (annual, not immediate).
- Critically: it applies to **FDA-pipeline investigational drugs past Phase I** — **not**
  to dietary supplements, **not** to off-label use of approved drugs for unapproved
  indications, and **not** to non-pipeline substances. So a substance like chlorine
  dioxide or an off-label antiparasitic for cancer is **outside** the Right to Try
  framework; including it in this corpus is *documentation, not legal cover.*

Stating this honestly protects the people the tool is for. "Right to try" is a real,
limited right — not a blanket permission, and we will not let it be read as one.

## The experimenters' voices — acknowledged, with their risks intact

People experiment — some within Right to Try, far more outside any framework, on their
own judgment and their own bodies. Those experiences are real, and the people are real.
The tool **does not moralize at them and does not abandon them** (per the substrate's
non-abandonment doctrine): it surfaces the evidence class and the dose-anchored harms
plainly, and leaves the choice where it belongs. **Non-abandonment is not
facilitation** — the tool never writes a protocol for a dangerous ingestion, never
cheerleads, and never hides a harm to please. It informs; it does not endorse.

## The aspiration (and it is *only* aspiration)

There may come a day when those experimenter voices find a method — **perhaps built by
Garrison Node** — to contribute their n=1 experiences into a **crowd-sourced,
open-sourced, cryptographically-attested** corpus: de-identified at the source, each
contribution hash-anchored, the aggregate able to surface a signal no single person
could see. That is the long horizon — the FUAG / data-vault / seed-record pattern
pointed at patient self-report. **It is not built, not promised, and not current
capability.** It is recorded here as direction, deferred to the mesh and to updates, so
the trajectory is legible without overclaiming the present.
